Music has changed so much in its lifetime in the last few decades. New genres have burst into the scene and gained extreme popularity while others faded away. New artists claimed their spot among the greats and commanded the attention of viewers with every new song. One of the biggest changes of the new era of music is how much data is being recorded on every song that releases so people can know what songs were popular and more in depth things like the tempo, beat loudness, and even time signatures. This allows creators to get feedback on what makes a great song and be able to refine their music to make the best possible product. This article will be showing some of the most notable things this dataset can tell us in visual form.
## ![Image 1](https://github.com/Owen-Smith4/CS130-Project/blob/main/img1.png)
The first visualization shows the number of songs that are in the dataset from each year in the 2000s. This is important because the 2000s were a very big year for music and marked a shift in which genres were popular and which were dying out. Pop music was growing dramatically during this decade and it is definitely reflected in the data as we will see later on. Lots of artists we all know and love today started their careers in the 2000s and made most of these most popular songs that are in this dataset. Some of the most popular songs from this era were "Yeah!" by Usher and Ludacris, "I Gotta Feeling" by The Black Eyed Peas, and "Hips Don't Lie" by Shakira.
## ![Image 2](https://github.com/Owen-Smith4/CS130-Project/blob/main/img2.png)
The second visualization shows the most popular genres out of the entirety of the dataset spanning between 1986 and 2010. The most popular genres were hip-hop, blues rock, and contemporary christian music. The data had to be filtered to having 60 or more songs in a genre to help remove hyper specific genres that have very few songs in them to get a better overview of the data. It is important to know which genres are the most popular so people can know what they should start listening to or even go to something more obscure to try to find something new that they have never heard of. You are able to see which genres have passed the test of time like hip-hop which has been popular all the way from the 1980s to now.
## ![Image 3](https://github.com/Owen-Smith4/CS130-Project/blob/main/img3.png)
The third visualization shows how many songs the most popular artists or groups have within this dataset. In order to find this they use a variable called artist hotness that calculates the popularity of an artist between 0 and 1.1 using things like streams and monthly listeners. The threshold to be considered one of the most popular artists for me was .85 or higher which was a very difficult group to get in. The most popular genre for producing the most trending artists is definitely hip-hop/pop. Usher, Rihanna, and Kanye West had a lot of songs ranked among the top including songs they made with other artists featured on them. The most popular artist was Kanye West scoring 1.08 as a hotness score.
##
Overall this dataset helps us get an understanding of how music has progressed through time and how analytics can continue to push music creation forward as far as it can go. It also helps us keep a record of the great artists and songs so they won't be forgotten. Finally, using things like artist hotness we can develop a easy way to rank artists and songs in different tiers based on what their score is. Hopefully in the future we get even more statistics that can be recorded to help make the dataset even better and also that more songs can be added to help it become more expansive.
